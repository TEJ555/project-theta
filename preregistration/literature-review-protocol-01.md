# Project Theta systematic scoping review protocol

## Administrative record

- Title: Scientific theories, indicators and research safeguards for artificial
  consciousness assessment
- Review type: systematic scoping review with narrative synthesis and evidence mapping
- Protocol frozen: 26 August 2026
- Coverage end date: 26 August 2026
- Repository: Project Theta
- Protocol version: 1.0

This protocol was frozen after preliminary searches, laboratory design work and the
first Claude experiments. It is not a prospective justification of those completed
experiments. It will be used to evaluate the existing design, identify changes and
guide future protocols. Any later amendment will be appended with a date and reason.

## Review questions

1. Which scientific theories of consciousness yield properties that can be expressed
   as computational or behavioural indicators in artificial systems?
2. What empirical support, criticism and unresolved disagreement bears on those
   theories and indicators?
3. How have researchers proposed assessing consciousness or consciousness-relevant
   properties in artificial systems?
4. Which confounds limit inferences from behaviour, self-report, architecture and
   simulation to phenomenal consciousness?
5. Which welfare, governance and communication safeguards are justified under
   uncertainty about artificial consciousness?
6. Which Project Theta components are supported, challenged or left unvalidated by
   the reviewed literature?

## Scope

The review covers scientific and philosophical work relevant to phenomenal, access,
self and state consciousness when it bears directly on testable artificial-system
properties. Theory families include global workspace, integrated information,
recurrent processing, higher-order representation, predictive processing,
interoceptive inference, attention schema, embodied and enactive cognition, and
computational functionalism. It also covers AI-specific indicator frameworks,
measurement methods, behavioural confounds, model self-report and welfare governance.

The review does not attempt to decide whether any current system is conscious. It does
not calculate a probability of consciousness or combine indicators into a score.

## Information sources

Formal searches will use PubMed, Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and arXiv.
Publisher and journal pages will be used to verify metadata and publication status.
Backward and forward citation searching will identify seminal and recent work missed
by keyword searches. Scopus and Web of Science are not available for this review and
this limitation will be reported.

## Search families

Searches will combine terms from the following families. Exact queries, dates and
result counts will be preserved in the search log.

1. Consciousness theory:
   `consciousness AND (global workspace OR integrated information OR recurrent
   processing OR higher order OR predictive processing OR attention schema)`
2. Artificial systems:
   `(artificial intelligence OR machine consciousness OR large language model) AND
   (consciousness indicators OR consciousness assessment OR sentience)`
3. Embodiment and interoception:
   `(consciousness OR self) AND (interoception OR embodiment OR active inference)`
4. Measurement validity:
   `(consciousness OR awareness) AND (no-report OR self-report OR metacognition OR
   behavioural marker OR construct validity)`
5. Welfare and governance:
   `(artificial intelligence OR artificial agent) AND (welfare OR moral patienthood OR
   consciousness research ethics)`

## Eligibility criteria

### Include

- peer-reviewed empirical studies, systematic reviews and major theory papers;
- scholarly books or chapters that introduced a theory central to the review;
- AI-specific reports or preprints with clear authorship, methods and falsifiable
  claims when peer-reviewed evidence is not yet available;
- responsible-research and welfare proposals directly addressing uncertainty about
  artificial consciousness;
- English-language work published or publicly posted by the coverage end date;
- earlier seminal work where needed to represent a theory accurately.

### Exclude

- marketing pages, anonymous essays, news coverage and unsupported opinion;
- anecdotal chatbot conversations offered as direct evidence of experience;
- work using the word consciousness only metaphorically;
- proposals with no assessable mechanism, indicator, argument or research safeguard;
- duplicate records, superseded manuscripts when a final peer-reviewed article is
  available, and inaccessible records whose claims cannot be checked.

## Screening and extraction

Records will be deduplicated by DOI, title and author. Title and abstract screening
will apply the eligibility criteria. Full-text or full-record screening will then
classify included sources by theory, evidence type and relevance to Project Theta.

The extraction table will record:

- citation and persistent identifier;
- publication status and year;
- theory or topic;
- study type, population or system, and sample where applicable;
- claimed consciousness-relevant property;
- empirical result or central argument;
- limitations and live objections;
- possible Project Theta operationalization;
- what the source does not license us to infer;
- inclusion decision and reason.

This first version uses one primary reviewer supported by reproducible searches and
machine-assisted record handling. A second independent human reviewer is required
before describing the review as publication-grade or claiming dual-screened coverage.

## Evidence appraisal

No single hierarchy can fairly rank neuroscience experiments, computational models
and philosophical arguments. Each included source will instead be labelled on relevant
dimensions:

- peer-reviewed, preprint, report, book or commentary;
- empirical, theoretical, methodological or normative;
- preregistered or not stated;
- direct test, indirect support, design proposal or criticism;
- replicated, multi-method, single-study or not applicable;
- major construct-validity and generalization limitations.

The review will distinguish evidence for a mechanism from evidence that the mechanism
is necessary or sufficient for phenomenal consciousness.

## Synthesis plan

The synthesis will be structured by question and theory family. It will compare areas
of agreement, disagreement and underdetermination. A theory-to-design matrix will map
sources to Project Theta components, manipulations, controls, outcomes, rival
explanations and required revisions.

No quantitative meta-analysis is planned because the included literatures do not share
a common population, intervention or outcome. Trial-level results will not be treated
as independent evidence about a theory. No consciousness score will be produced.

## Reporting commitments

The repository will include the protocol, exact search log, evidence table, narrative
review, bibliography, excluded-record categories, limitations and amendment history.
Preprints will be labelled. Null and theory-challenging findings will not be omitted.
The distinction between behavioural indicators, computational indicators and
phenomenal consciousness will be maintained throughout.
