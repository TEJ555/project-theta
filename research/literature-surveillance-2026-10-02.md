# Literature surveillance update, 2 October 2026

## Status

This is a search update after the frozen scoping review. It is not a retrospective
amendment to the review protocol, a second-reviewer screen, or a claim of exhaustive
coverage. Sources remain candidates until their full text and references are screened
against the registered eligibility criteria.

The update was performed without changing the active V10 protocol or progression rules.

## High-priority records

### Palminteri and Wu, 2026

[Beyond computational equivalence: the behavioral inference principle for machine
consciousness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12907924/) is a peer-reviewed,
open-access methodological article in *Neuroscience of Consciousness*.

The authors reject simple computational equivalence as a practical attribution rule for
opaque AI systems. They propose inferring latent computational processes from stringent,
theory-informed behavioural patterns while explicitly accounting for priors, mimicry,
and architectural opacity.

Relevance to Project Theta:

- supports treating behaviour as evidence for a narrow latent process rather than as
  direct evidence of phenomenology
- supports adversarial tests, held-out generalisation, longitudinal behaviour, and
  explicit rival models
- strengthens the case for reporting training priors, prompt scaffolds, and wrapper
  mechanisms as possible explanations
- does not license a consciousness classification from V10 performance

### Pennartz, 2026

[How can we validate theory-derived indicators of consciousness in Artificial
Intelligence?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41820112/) is a peer-reviewed letter in
*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2026.01.011`.

The accessible bibliographic record contains no abstract. Full-text screening is
required before extracting its argument or using it to change the evidence map. Its
title and placement in the indicator debate make it a priority record.

### Butlin and colleagues, 2026

[Consciousness indicators, mimicry, and internal
variants](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42036253/) is a peer-reviewed reply in
*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2026.04.006`.

The accessible bibliographic record contains no abstract. Full-text screening is
required. The record's keywords directly match two central Theta validity risks:
behavioural mimicry and uncertainty about which internal implementation, if any, a
behavioural result supports.

### Chandaria and colleagues, 2026

[From cacophony to hierarchy: a principled framework for assessing AI
consciousness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35618) was posted on 28 September 2026, after
the frozen review's coverage date. It is a preprint and has not been treated as
peer-reviewed evidence.

The paper proposes a hierarchy of operational indicators and a Bayesian aggregation
framework. It should be screened as a possible synthesis of the field. Project Theta
should not adopt a consciousness probability or aggregate score merely because such a
framework exists. Calibration, theory dependence, negative indicators, and sensitivity
to correlated evidence require separate review.

## Immediate implications

1. Keep behavioural, computational, and phenomenal claims separate.
2. Describe V10 as a reliability test of a composite regulation task, not as a
   consciousness assessment.
3. Treat flexible held-out behaviour as evidence only after checking simple policies,
   prompt leakage, training priors, and wrapper-supplied state.
4. Add an open-weight model route when feasible so internal variants can be studied with
   activation analysis and causal intervention.
5. Seek a human second reviewer for these records and the existing evidence table.

## Search limitations

This was a targeted surveillance search, not a rerun of all registered databases.
Publisher access prevented full-text extraction for the two *Trends in Cognitive
Sciences* letters. No substantive claim from those letters has been added to the frozen
narrative review without full-text verification.
